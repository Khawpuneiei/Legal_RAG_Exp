# Start of a business and pre-commencement expenses

## Where to look

- Actual commencement: Date of Commencement guide paras 2.1, 4.1 to 4.4. Industry examples: its Annex, possibly labelled para 6 (Updates and amendments); spot it by industry names and dated event lists.
- Bar on expenses before commencement: section 14(1); same guide paras 2.3, 3.2, 4.5.
- Deemed commencement (first-dollar concession): section 14R; same guide paras 3.3 to 3.5; Pre-commencement guide paras 2.2, 3.7, 4.2.
- IP, R&D, renovation, design and innovation spending: sections 14A(3), 14C(2), 14N(4), 14P(5), 14EA(8), 14EB(12); Pre-commencement guide paras 2.1, 4.1.
- Attribution to income streams: section 14X; Pre-commencement guide paras 2.3, 2.4, 5.1, 6.1 to 6.5, 7.1.
- Worked examples: Pre-commencement guide Annexes A and B.

## Check before answering

- Actual or deemed commencement? Keep the two dates apart.
- Which event made the profit-making structure ready and began commercial activity?
- Actual date: use the first receipt only if the industry test points there. Deemed date: the first receipt fixes the accounting year.
- Raise para 3.5 only if the facts point to an earlier actual start.
- Is the business excluded by section 14R(4)?
- IP, R&D, renovation, design or innovation spending? Take the date from its deeming subsection.
- Expense incurred on or after the s 14X(1)(b)(ii) and (2)(b)(ii) cut-off date? It is a fixed calendar date, not the business start. If not, see para 5.1.
- Does first-year income mix streams, or hold only one non-normal type (section 14X(4))?
- More than one trade? See para 6.3.

## How to work it out

Actual commencement:
1. List events in date order.
2. Mark each preparatory, operational or ceremonial, per the industry's Annex example.
3. The first operational event is the date. Earlier expenses fail section 14(1) unless a deeming rule applies.

Deemed date:
1. Find the deemed date as section 14R(2)(b) and para 3.3 define it.
2. Band each expense: (a) before the s 14R(2)(a) look-back period, counted from the deemed date, not the receipt: not deductible under s 14R; (b) inside that period, before the deemed date: s 14R(2)(c) applies; (c) on or after the deemed date, before the first receipt: s 14R(1) applies; (d) on or after the first receipt: ordinary rules.
3. State the date each band is treated as incurred; if asked when, put it first.

Attribution (section 14X):
1. Set directly attributable expenses against their own stream.
2. Apportion the rest by income proportion, naming the para 6.1 bases.
3. Treat any excess as a trade loss (para 6.5).

Keep event lists and bands in your reasoning. Reply with the result first, then one line per expense or event with its [n].
